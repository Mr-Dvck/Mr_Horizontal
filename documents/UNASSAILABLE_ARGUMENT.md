## The Horizon and Geometry of Sight

### The Claim

The spherical model claims the Earth is a sphere with a radius of 3,959 miles.
This would cause the horizon to drop below eye level according to the formula:

```
h = d² / (2R)
```

Where:
- h = drop height
- d = distance to horizon
- R = Earth's radius (3,959 miles)

### The Reality

**At 3 miles (typical horizon distance for a 6-foot observer):**
```
h = 3² / (2 × 3959) = 9 / 7918 = 0.00114 miles = 6 inches
```

**What do we observe?** The horizon is always at eye level. There is no drop.

**At 10 miles:**
```
h = 10² / (2 × 3959) = 100 / 7918 = 0.0126 miles = 66 feet
```

**What do we observe?** The horizon is still at eye level. There is no 66-foot drop.

### The Clincher

If the Earth were a sphere, you should be able to see London from New York with
a powerful enough telescope. The line of sight would pass hundreds of miles above
the Earth's surface.

**Yet no such visibility is possible. Why?** Because the Earth is flat, and the
horizon is a perspective limit, not a physical curve.

### The sailors' "knowledge" is not evidence—it's tradition

For thousands of years, people believed the Earth was flat, then spherical, then
round. What changed wasn't better observation—it was better indoctrination.

The "bottom-first disappearance" of ships is explained perfectly by perspective
and atmospheric lensing on a flat plane. There is no physical "bulge" because
there is no curve.

## Celestial Mechanics and the Local Sun

### The Claim

The spherical model claims the Sun is 93 million miles away and moves in an
elliptical orbit around the Earth.

### The Reality

**Angular size calculation:**
```
θ = 2 × arctan(D / (2 × d))
```

Where:
- θ = angular size
- D = diameter of Sun (864,000 miles)
- d = distance to Sun

**At 93 million miles:**
```
θ = 2 × arctan(864000 / (2 × 93000000)) = 0.53 degrees
```

**The problem:** If the Sun is 93 million miles away, it should change angular
size significantly throughout the day. At noon, the Sun is closest. At
sunrise/sunset, the Sun is much farther away.

**What do we observe?** The Sun maintains a constant angular size of 0.5 degrees
throughout the day. There is no measurable change.

### The Flat Earth Explanation

The Sun moves in a circular path above the plane, maintaining a relatively
constant distance from any given observer during daylight hours. The apparent
size change is minimal and within the range of atmospheric distortion.

### The Southern Cross Question

On a flat Earth with the South Pole at the center, the Southern Cross would be
visible from all locations south of the equator, looking toward the center. The
constellation appears in roughly the same position in the sky for observers in
South America, South Africa, and Australia because they are all looking toward
the same central point—the South Pole.

This is exactly what we observe.

### The Spherical Model's Problem

On a globe, the Sun is 93 million miles away, yet it maintains the same angular
size throughout the day. This requires the Sun to move in a perfect circle at a
constant distance—a feat of celestial engineering that defies all known physics.

The spherical model cannot explain why the Sun doesn't appear larger at noon and
smaller at sunset if it's moving in an elliptical orbit.

Furthermore, the spherical model cannot explain why the Sun sets at different
times in different locations. If the Sun is 93 million miles away and illuminates
half the Earth at once, the transition from day to night should be gradual and
uniform across vast regions—not the sharp, localized day/night line we observe.

5. **Conclusion** - Accept, reject, or refine the hypothesis
